One platform, two operators

The owners want ROUTIQ to take the useful parts of the reference app (customers, stock and purchasing) without becoming the reference app. The direction: one core every workspace gets, a few modules switched on per workspace, and a preset (trucking or bus) that picks the defaults and the words. A trucking company moving goods and a bus company moving people keep their own core work, and share the same customer, stock and money screens.

Customers

Who is a customer?

The biggest design question. A trucking customer is a company that books jobs. A bus company has three kinds of customer, and only two of them fit ROUTIQ today.

Account (company)

Trucking: shippers who book haulage (a cement plant, a brewery, an importer). Bus: companies, schools and NGOs with charter or staff-transport contracts.

Record with contacts, agreed rates, trips, what they owe, follow-ups. Same screen for both presets.

Individual (phone number)

Bus: people who send or collect parcels, and regular travellers. Trucking: small one-off customers paying cash.

Found by phone number at the counter, created in two fields. Light record: parcels, payments, notes.

Passenger (ticket)

Bus only: everyone who buys a seat. Thousands per week, seat maps, departures, cash per counter.

This is ticketing, deferred on purpose (ARCHITECTURE §13, decided 22 Jul): revenue keeps coming in through daily cash sessions. If ticketing is built later, a passenger's phone number links to an Individual.

Recommendation

One Customers module with two types, Account and Individual, built on the existing Party concept, so a supplier and a customer can even be the same company. Same list, same record layout and same actions in both presets, with the preset deciding the default type and the words. Passengers stay out until ticketing is decided.

The other side

  • Bus owners may expect "customers" to mean passengers. Showing a customer list without passengers could disappoint; say it clearly at sale time.
  • Individuals at parcel volume (hundreds a week) make a big list; the list defaults to Accounts, and Individuals are reached by phone search.
  • "What they owe" needs revenue entries with a due date and a "paid" link, which ROUTIQ doesn't have yet (decision below).

What changes with the bus preset

  • Words: Journeys instead of Trips; Contract tab instead of Rates (a monthly or per-journey contract price).
  • For an Individual (parcel customer), the record is lighter: phone number, parcels sent and received, payments, notes. No contract, no account manager.
  • Layout, actions, status block and tabs follow the same shell, so staff who use both presets learn it once.
Customer relations

Follow-ups and complaints (the "CRM" part)

Kept small on purpose: no sales pipeline, no campaigns. Staff log what happened with a customer, set a next step, and complaints get an owner until resolved. Due follow-ups land in "Needs you" on Home.

Rules

  • Three kinds of note: call, visit, complaint. Each can set a follow-up date and an assignee.
  • Complaints have an owner and a status (open → resolved, with what was agreed). A money outcome (credit, refund) is a normal entry in Money, linked.
  • Notes are append-only, like vehicle notes.
  • Due follow-ups and open complaints count on the Customers row and show in "Needs you".

Parcel rules and screens

  • Parcels page (Module list): the five-step strip above as tiles that filter; list rows with code, receiver, destination, status, fee.
  • Receive a parcel (Record layout, phone first): the sender's phone number finds or creates the Individual customer; fee from the route's parcel rate.
  • Load on a journey (Line items): pick the departing journey, scan or tick parcels; the journey shows "41 parcels on board".
  • Hand over (Decision): receiver gives the code; mark collected, payment if pay-on-collection.
  • Damaged or lost: a complaint on the customer, linked to the parcel and the journey.
  • Each parcel fee is revenue that reaches the agency's daily cash session; ROUTIQ doesn't invoice.
  • Later, with the Notifications module: SMS to the receiver on arrival.

Chart spot

  • Parcels per day and parcel revenue per route (managers, executives).
  • Average days from arrival to collection.

Rules for role scope

  • The sidebar, the phone bar, Home's "Needs you", tile sets, chart visibility and each page's primary button all come from one role definition (data, tested per role).
  • A role-forbidden action is absent. A state-blocked action is shown locked with the reason ("Use parts: none left in this magasin").
  • Branch scope narrows every list and count to the person's agencies; Direction sees all and gets the branch comparison charts.
  • Drivers see only their own vehicle, trips, expenses and problems; they never see other people's money.
  • Counts only include work this role can act on (a Technician's Stock count is deliveries to receive, not approvals).

Same role, two presets

  • Cashier, bus: the parcel counter is the main job; cash in and out of the agency.
  • Cashier, trucking: payments received from customers and cash expenses; no parcels unless groupage is on.
  • Driver, bus: journeys and confirming parcels on board. Driver, trucking: trips, delivery note photo as proof of delivery.
  • Direction, Administrator, Finance and Technician work the same way in both presets; only the words change.

Ask back

Decisions needed before building

Product

  1. Customers = Accounts + Individuals, passengers out until ticketing? Recommended: yes.
  2. Parcels as a module, on by default for bus? Recommended: yes; it's the bus agencies' second income and already part of their daily cash.
  3. Lightweight CRM only (notes, follow-ups, complaints), no sales pipeline? Recommended: yes.
  4. Which comes first: Customers, Parcels or Stock? Recommended: Customers (both presets use it, and Parcels needs it), then Parcels for bus pilots, then Stock with the workshop.

Architecture (needs an ADR each)

  1. "What they owe": revenue entries need a due date and a link to payments received. Today ROUTIQ records revenue as earned, with no "unpaid" state. Smallest version: a receivable flag + payment-received entries that settle it.
  2. Party model: one table for customers and suppliers with roles (as ARCHITECTURE.md lists), or separate tables? Recommended: one, as planned.
  3. New module codes: CUSTOMERS, PARCELS, INVENTORY (already listed), each owning its commands, reads and sidebar rows.
  4. Starter packs per preset: default customer types, parcel rates, note kinds, seeded at setup like categories.
