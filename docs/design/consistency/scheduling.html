Scheduling

A customer calls: "I need a truck on Thursday, Douala to Yaoundé, 30 tonnes of cement." Today ROUTIQ can only record a trip once it has started. Scheduling adds the step before: book the trip for a date, put it on a calendar, assign a truck and a driver, and let the driver see in their schedule who booked it and what it involves.

Direction

The direction for trucking (and bus)

One trip, one more status

No separate "booking" object. A booked trip is the same trip record, with a new first status: Planned → In progress → Closed (or Cancelled). Booking creates a planned trip; starting it on the day turns it into the trip the driver already records today. Everything that already exists (crew, readings, expenses, closing) keeps working.

Why not a separate booking

  • Two records for one job means copying data and two places to cancel.
  • The customer, rate and route belong on the trip anyway, for revenue and what the customer owes.
  • The trucking and bus presets keep their own words (trip vs journey) on one shape.
Placement

Where planning lives: a tab inside Trips, not a new sidebar row

Planning is a view of trips (planned ones), so it lives on the Trips page as its first tab. The sidebar keeps one Trips row, whose count is "trips to assign". The one button, Book a trip, sits in the Trips header on every tab, on Home for dispatchers, and on a customer record.

How you get there

  • Sidebar → Trips (lands on Planning for dispatchers, on All trips for Finance).
  • Home → "Planned this week" card → Planning.
  • Customer → Trips tab → "Upcoming" → Planning filtered to that customer.
  • Truck → its planned trips next to maintenance in its Overview.

Why not a "Planning" sidebar row

  • One place for trips: planned, running and closed are the same record at different steps.
  • Keeps the sidebar at its size; module rows only appear when a module is on.
  • If the Scheduling module is off, the Planning tab simply isn't there and Trips works as today.

  • Click a chip → the trip in the record panel.
  • Filters narrow to one truck, driver or customer (a customer's month = what to tell them on the phone).

What the driver can do

  • Planned: see details, call the customer, start the trip on the day.
  • In progress: log fuel or an expense, report a problem, add a note, record the delivery (who received it, optional photo of the signed delivery note).
  • After delivery: the trip is still in progress; close it with the arrival reading.
  • One primary button at a time: the next thing the trip needs.

Rules

  • The step line is the same everywhere (board, trip page, driver), so office and driver talk about the same thing.
  • Delivered is a recorded fact, not a status (decided 2026-10-08). Recording it stores the time, who received the goods and an optional photo on the trip or its last stage; the trip stays In progress until it is closed. Statuses stay Planned, In progress, Closed, Cancelled.
  • Drivers see their own trips only, without the trip price. The company setting "Show trip price to drivers" is off by default. When the driver collects cash from the customer, the trip shows "Amount to collect" instead (decided 2026-10-08).
  • Facts queue offline and sync later: starting a trip and recording a delivery. Cancelling, moving or reassigning needs the server (ADR-0012 §5). Queueing waits for the offline outbox (#45).

Rules

  • Opens from: Planning (button or click on an empty day), a customer record ("New trip for this customer"), and Home.
  • Truck and driver pickers show availability for that date: free, already booked (with which trip), grounded or in maintenance (a warning with the reason; booking still goes ahead, ADR-0012 §4). Sold or retired trucks and drivers who can't drive aren't offered.
  • Double-booking is a warning, not a block (warn, don't block), but the board marks it in amber until resolved.
  • After booking: Move changes the date, Assign changes the truck or driver, Edit fixes the customer, route or price (ADR-0012 §3). Each is a command with history and needs the server. A move or a new truck or driver shows "changed" on the driver's card (ADR-0012 §7).
  • Cancelling a planned trip is a decision dialog with a reason; it stays in the customer's history as cancelled.
  • Recurring bookings ("every Monday for 6 weeks") create one planned trip per date, each editable.

Rules

  • The driver's "Trips" tab opens on My schedule: today, tomorrow, then the next days, as an agenda (never a grid on phone).
  • Each card answers what the driver asks: where, when, for whom (with a Call link), what cargo, which truck, who booked it.
  • Start trip unlocks on the day; starting asks only the odometer and confirms the crew.
  • Changes show as "Changed" with what moved and who moved it, until the driver opens the card.
  • Offline: the schedule is cached from the last time the phone was online; starting a trip or recording a delivery offline is a fact, accepted later. A start on a trip the office cancelled meanwhile still goes ahead and is flagged; a start on a trip someone else already started is refused (ADR-0012 §5). Needs the offline outbox (#45).
  • Drivers see only their own trips, never other drivers'. The trip price stays hidden unless the company turns on "Show trip price to drivers"; a trip where the driver collects cash shows "Amount to collect".
Bus preset

Bus: timetable and charters

A bus agency schedules differently: most journeys repeat on a timetable (Douala → Yaoundé at 06:00, 10:00, 14:00 every day). The timetable creates each day's planned journeys; the dispatcher only assigns buses and drivers. Charters (a school, a wedding, a company) are booked one by one, exactly like a trucking trip.

Timetable (Company setting, Administrator)

  • Route, departure times, days of the week, default bus type. Saved as data; each night it creates the next days' planned journeys.
  • Changing the timetable only affects journeys not yet planned. Holidays are a list of skip dates.
  • Seats and tickets stay out (ticketing deferred); a journey may record passengers count and cash at close, as today.

Same parts as trucking

  • Charters use Book a trip with the customer account (school, company) and agreed price.
  • Drivers get the same "My schedule".
  • Parcels to load show on the departure ("23 parcels to load"), linking to the Parcels loading form.
