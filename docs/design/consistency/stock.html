Stock and purchasing

The two Loom videos are tutorials for the reference app, a separate business-management app, recorded in April 2025. Video 2's description says "replicate this process for our upcoming project". That process is purchasing and stock: buy parts with a purchase order, approve it, receive the goods into a store, pay, then track stock per store, move it between stores, and adjust it. ROUTIQ already plans a Stock module for workshop parts (#27, with decisions recorded). This page maps the reference flow onto it and draws the screens in ROUTIQ's consistency system.

Decided 2026-10-08

  • Pilot scope: receipts only. No purchase orders yet. The supplier invoice is an expense in Money and goes through the existing approval chain; a purchase order would approve the same money twice. The purchase order screens below are kept for later, when a company wants approval before ordering.
  • Approvals: no new rule. Supplier invoices use the approval thresholds in Company settings. Adjustments are approved by someone other than the counter (#27).
  • Fuel is not stock for the pilot. Fuel bought at a station stays an expense on the truck. A company with its own tank gets the tank as a magasin holding litres, in a later slice.

Workflow

The workflow in ROUTIQ

1 · RequestWorkshop lead raises a PO: supplier, magasin, lines (article, qty, price).Workshop · phone or desktop
→
2 · ApproveWithin the auto band it's approved at once; above it, it waits for an approver.Admin / fleet manager
→
3 · ReceiveWhen the goods arrive: received quantities per line (partial OK), delivery note photo. Stock goes up.Storekeeper / workshop · phone
→
4 · Invoice → MoneyThe supplier invoice is recorded as an expense linked to the PO, approved in Money like any expense.Finance
→
5 · Use on a truckParts go out to a work order (Sortie); their cost lands on that truck. Transfers and adjustments as needed.Workshop

A PO closes by itself when everything is received and invoiced. Like every approved record, it is never edited afterwards; a mistake is corrected by a reversing movement.

Why it looks like this

  • Receiving happens in the magasin, on a phone, often with gloves on: big −/+ steppers prefilled with the ordered quantity, so the common case is one tap.
  • Anything short is shown in amber, with what happens next.
  • Works offline: it's a fact (goods arrived), so it's accepted later with a flag if needed. Approvals stay online, as for everything else in ROUTIQ.

Overview and charts

Tiles for everyone on Stock

  • Stock value · Below minimum · To receive · Negative balance (each one filters the list).
  • On Purchase orders: Waiting approval · Ordered, not received · Received, not invoiced · Spent this month.

Charts for managers and executives

  • Purchasing per month (columns, 12 months).
  • Top articles by spend (sorted bars), and parts cost per truck, which joins the executive "Top trucks by cost".
  • Stock value over time (line). Needs a monthly valuation snapshot.

The reference app's overview showed Requested / Approved / Purchased / Paid totals; in ROUTIQ those become the purchase-order tiles above, and "Paid" comes from Money.

Ask back

Questions for the owners

Scope

  1. Both videos are tutorials for another business app. Is "the upcoming project" ROUTIQ's parts and stock module, or a different product?
  2. Is video 1 (customers) part of the ask? If yes: haulage customers and what they owe?
  3. Purchase orders were not in the #27 plan (receipts only). Do you want approval before spending (PO), or is recording receipts enough for the pilot? Decided: receipts only for the pilot.

Rules

  1. Who may approve a PO, and from what amount? (Reuses the approval bands.) Decided: no PO yet; supplier invoices use the existing thresholds.
  2. Partial receipts and partial invoices: needed from day one?
  3. Transfers between branches: does the destination confirm receipt (in-transit stock)?
  4. Minimum stock per article: per magasin or one number per article?
  5. Should fuel be an article (bulk diesel in a tank) or stay as fuel expenses? Decided: fuel expenses; a tank becomes a magasin later, only for a company that owns one.
