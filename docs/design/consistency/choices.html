Keep neutral

For
  • Already shipped, tested, in every video and the tester handbook. Zero migration.
  • Colour is reserved for status, so red "Grounded" and amber "Waiting" stand out more than anywhere else.
  • Stock shadcn neutral reads well on cheap screens in sunlight; high contrast everywhere.
  • Keeps the decision open for a stronger direction later (the owners called the brand pass "not creative enough").
Against
  • No identity: it looks like any shadcn template, in demos and to partners.
  • The owners' own brief (27 Sept) asked for identity from the logo; this leaves that undone.
  • The rounded font only exists on Apple devices; the pilot's Android phones fall back anyway, so desktop and phone already look different.

Brand navy Recommended

For
  • The product looks like ROUTIQ: logo colours, a navy rail that separates navigation from work, one blue accent for "you are here" and focus.
  • Neutrals tinted toward the navy so grey never looks like another product; system font is Roboto on the pilot phones, nothing to download.
  • Comes with guard tests already written (no monospace figures, no uppercase labels, no colour literals, logo only from one component).
  • Cheap if ported as tokens + logo only: one file of variables and one component, not the 89-file branch.
Against
  • The owners reviewed this exact pass and asked for something more creative. Picking it may feel like going backwards.
  • Brand blue sits close to the info-blue status; "In progress" badges need a different hue so they don't read as links.
  • Every screenshot, video and handbook page changes again.
  • Grey group labels on navy (55% white) are weaker in direct sun.
Why I pick brand navy: identity was the owners' own request, and the cost is one small PR when limited to tokens, logo and icons. The structure rules in this proposal work in either theme, so this choice doesn't block anything. What would change my mind: if the owners still find navy generic, keep neutral for now and pick colours from one of the design-lab directions later. Either way, the logo should ship now.

2 · Record pages: one shell for every record?

A truck already opens as identity → status → facts → tabs → action bar. Should a trip and a finance entry open the same way? See the drawing.

One shell for every record Recommended

For
  • Users learn one way to read a record: status on top, who acts, numbers, then tabs.
  • The shell, record panel and action bar already exist and are tested on the truck.
  • New features land as a tab or a panel, not as a new page design.
Against
  • A finance entry is small; seven tabs would be mostly empty. Needs a "tabs only where there is content" rule.
  • The two largest slices in the fix list (trip detail, entry detail).
  • Finance people may prefer a dense ledger-style detail to a workspace.

Tidy each detail page separately

For
  • Each page fits its data; an entry stays a compact sheet.
  • Less rework now; fixes ship faster.
Against
  • Three different detail styles is exactly today's inconsistency, and it grows with each new record type.
  • The same entry would still look different from Finance and from the truck.
Why one shell: the owners asked for the same reading order everywhere. The fix for "small records" is to show only the tabs that have content (entry: Overview, History; trip: Overview, Legs, Money, History). Identity, status, facts and the action bar always appear.
Owner pick
✓ One shellPer type

3 · Where do forms open?

Today the same "report a problem" form opens as a centred dialog from Maintenance and as a side panel from the truck. See the surfaces.

Side panel by default Recommended

For
  • The page behind stays visible: you see the truck you are recording against.
  • Room for 14 fields; scrolls well; on phone it becomes a full sheet with the same layout.
  • 16 forms already use it; the back link returns to the record you came from.
Against
  • Covers a third of a 1280 px laptop screen.
  • A form inside a form (register a person while recording a trip) needs a panel stack.

Centred dialog for short forms

For
  • Familiar and focused; Settings already use it.
  • Good for 2 to 4 fields.
Against
  • Cramped above 6 fields; scrolling inside a dialog on phone is poor.
  • Loses the signal "a dialog means a decision".
  • "Short" is a judgment call, so the drift comes back.

A page per form

For
  • Deep links, all the room, drafts are natural.
Against
  • Loses context; slow for quick entries like fuel or a note.
  • More navigation and back-button traps.
Why the panel: most forms record a fact about something on screen, and the panel keeps that thing in view. Dialogs stay for decisions so they keep meaning "stop and decide". Pages stay for the two long captures.
Owner pick

4 · Fixed form layouts, or guidelines?

Six layouts (Quick entry, Record, Line items, Decision, Long capture, Edit in place) chosen by the form's type. See the layouts.

Six fixed layouts Recommended

For
  • A new form is a field list + a layout name: faster to build, same result every time.
  • Users meet the same arrangement in the same kind of form.
  • Tests can count fields and groups per layout and catch drift.
Against
  • Edge cases get squeezed (closing a trip has a date and a reason: Decision or Record?).
  • Needs the FormLayout component and field kit built first (slice 8, the largest).
  • Can feel rigid to whoever designs the next feature.

Guidelines, free composition

For
  • Flexible; no new component; each form tuned to its job.
Against
  • This is how today's forms ended up with 32 px and 44 px fields in one form, three button orders and two techniques.
  • Every review re-argues layout.
Why fixed: you asked for forms that are faster to build and predictable to use; guidelines alone have already failed once. The escape hatch: a form that truly fits none of the six adds a seventh layout to this page in its PR, so the system grows on purpose.
Owner pick
✓ FixedGuidelines

5 · Button order: submit last or submit first?

Today dialogs put Cancel then Submit on the right; panels put Submit then Cancel on the left. Either rule is fine; mixing them is not.

Submit last (right) Recommended

For
  • Matches dialogs today and the platform convention people know from Android and desktop dialogs.
  • The eye ends on the action; the hint on the left reads first.
  • On phone, submit sits under the right thumb (most users).
Against
  • 16 panel forms change.
  • On wide screens a right-aligned button is far from the fields on the left.

Submit first (left)

For
  • GOV.UK-style research on long forms: the primary button sits where the eye finishes the last field.
  • Panels already do it.
Against
  • Dialogs flip, and they then differ from the phone's own system dialogs.
  • Cancel ends up at the far right, where people expect the action.
Why submit last: it matches what users already see in system dialogs. The panel's narrow width (440 px) keeps the button near the fields anyway. To be honest, this one is close: consistency matters more than which side wins.
Owner pick
✓ Submit lastSubmit first

6 · Long forms on phone: steps, or one scroll?

Only Record a trip and Register a truck. See both layouts.

Steps + review Recommended

For
  • One section on a small screen at a time; the keyboard never hides the next required field.
  • The review step catches mistakes before an offline replay sends them.
  • Progress and drafts per step suit patchy connections.
Against
  • More taps; experienced users are slower.
  • Jumping between sections needs a step menu.
  • One definition has to render two ways (more code).

One long scroll

For
  • What exists today; simplest; fast for experts.
Against
  • Long scroll at 390 px with the keyboard open; required fields far down get missed.
  • No natural place to review.
Why steps: the people filling these on phones are drivers on low-end Android, often offline. For them, fewer mistakes beat fewer taps. A "Review" link on every step lets experienced users jump to the end.
Owner pick
✓ StepsOne scroll

8 · Phone navigation: bottom bar, or hamburger only?

See both.

Bottom bar: 3 places per role + Menu Recommended

For
  • Daily places in thumb reach, one tap; how the Android apps people use every day work.
  • Counts visible without opening anything.
  • A driver's bar can be "Home, My truck, Trips": the app feels made for them.
Against
  • Uses 62 px of a small screen, and stacks with the truck's action bar.
  • Picking the three per role is a product decision to maintain.
  • A new component and its tests.

Hamburger only (today)

For
  • Maximum screen for content; one navigation model; no work.
Against
  • Two taps to anything; hidden places get forgotten; counts invisible.
Why the bar: most daily use is on phones. The bar hides while a form is open, so it doesn't fight the form footer.

10 · Overview strips: on every list, or only some?

The owners' rule is "overview first". Today only Trucks has a strip. See the list page.

Every module list and queue Recommended

For
  • Same anatomy everywhere; the owners' rule applied literally.
  • Each number is also a filter, so it's a shortcut, not decoration.
Against
  • Pushes the table down on phone (two rows of tiles).
  • Needs a server summary read per list (API work on 4 lists).
  • Risk of vanity numbers if nobody owns what each one means.

Only where obviously useful

For
  • Less to build; tables appear sooner.
Against
  • "Obviously useful" is a judgment call; the inconsistency returns.
Why every list: it's what the owners asked for. Rules keep it honest: 2 to 4 numbers, each one filters the table, settings lists get a sentence instead, and on phone the strip shrinks to one row of chips.
